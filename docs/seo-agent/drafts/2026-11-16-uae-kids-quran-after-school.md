# Online Quran classes for kids in the UAE: a school-week plan

**Short answer:** Most families in the UAE fit a child's online Quran class into a fixed slot after school or in the early evening, two to five days a week, using Gulf Standard Time (GST, UTC+4). The UAE does not change its clocks, so a slot you agree once stays at the same local time all year. Short, regular lessons usually work better for young children than one long weekly session.

## Start with the school week, not the timetable

Before you ask for a lesson time, write down your child's real week:

- when school finishes and when your child is home;
- after-school activities, tuition and sports;
- homework time and dinner;
- a sensible bedtime on school nights.

The best Quran slot is the one your child can keep every week without rushing. For many families that is a quiet window after a short break and a snack, not straight after the school bus.

## How many lessons a week?

There is no single right answer, but these patterns are common:

| Learner | Typical pattern | Lesson length |
|---|---|---|
| Young beginner (Noorani Qaida) | 3–5 short lessons a week | 30 minutes |
| Reading the Quran with Tajweed | 3–4 lessons a week | 30 minutes |
| Hifz (memorisation) | 5 lessons a week | 30 minutes or longer, as agreed with the teacher |

## Working with Gulf Standard Time

The UAE uses GST all year, so your lesson time does not move. Some other countries do change their clocks, which matters if your teacher or a second parent is in a different time zone.

When you request a trial, give:

- your preferred days;
- two or three possible times in GST, for example "Monday and Wednesday, 6:30 pm GST";
- your child's age and what they can already read.

## Weekends and school holidays

Many families keep a lighter Quran routine at weekends and during school holidays, for example two review lessons instead of four new-lesson days. Tell the coordinator in advance if you travel during the long summer break or around Eid. A teacher can usually suggest review work so that progress is not lost.

## Make the online lesson easy for a child

A little setup makes a big difference:

1. **Same place every time.** A quiet corner with a table, not a sofa or bed.
2. **Headphones with a microphone**, so the teacher hears pronunciation clearly.
3. **Device charged and logged in five minutes early.**
4. **The book or Qaida open** at the last page studied.
5. **A parent nearby for the first few lessons**, especially for children under eight.

Our guide to [setting up an accessible online Quran lesson](/blog/online-quran-lesson-setup-accessibility) covers this in more detail.

## Female teacher requests

Many parents in the UAE ask for a female teacher, especially for girls. You can state this preference when you request a trial. Availability is confirmed before enrolment.

## Track progress without pressure

Ask the teacher for a short note after each lesson, or a weekly summary, covering what was taught, what to practise and anything to watch. Five to ten minutes of daily practice between lessons, with a parent listening, helps young learners more than long weekend catch-ups.
