# Online Tajweed classes for adults in the UAE: getting started

**Short answer:** Adults in the UAE usually start online Tajweed with a short level check, then two to four 30-minute one-to-one lessons a week at a fixed time in Gulf Standard Time. The fastest progress usually comes from a daily practice habit of about ten minutes, not from long lessons.

## Who this guide is for

- Adults who can read Arabic letters but want to recite correctly.
- Adults who learnt as children and want to correct old habits.
- Complete beginners who need to start with the letters first.

If you cannot yet read the Arabic letters, begin with [Noorani Qaida](/courses/noorani-qaida). If you can read slowly, [Quran reading with Tajweed](/courses/quran-nazara) is the usual starting course.

## Step 1: Find your real starting level

Before your first lesson, try reading a short passage aloud and note:

- letters you hesitate on;
- whether you can join letters smoothly;
- whether you know where to stop and pause.

There is no need to be embarrassed. A good teacher hears this in the first minutes of the trial and plans from there.

## Step 2: Choose a time you can protect

Working adults in the UAE often choose:

- **early morning** before work;
- **lunch break**, for a 30-minute lesson from a quiet room;
- **late evening**, after family time.

The UAE stays on Gulf Standard Time (UTC+4) all year, so an agreed slot does not move. Give the coordinator two or three times that you can keep every week.

The coordinator confirms which of your preferred times a teacher can offer before lessons are scheduled.

## Step 3: Know what Tajweed lessons cover

Courses usually build up in this order. The exact plan depends on your level and your teacher.

1. Correct pronunciation of each letter (makharij) and its qualities (sifaat).
2. Rules of noon saakin and tanween, and meem saakin.
3. Elongation (madd) and its types.
4. Stopping and starting correctly (waqf and ibtida).
5. Fluent recitation of longer passages with the rules applied.

## Step 4: Practise between lessons

Ten minutes a day is more useful than one long session at the weekend:

- re-read the lines from your last lesson;
- record yourself on your phone and compare with your teacher's correction;
- write down one rule you found hard and ask about it next lesson.
