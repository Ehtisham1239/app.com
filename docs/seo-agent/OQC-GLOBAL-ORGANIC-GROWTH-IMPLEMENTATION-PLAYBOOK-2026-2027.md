# OQC Global Organic Growth & Admissions Operating Playbook (2026–2027)

**Owner:** Online Quran College (OQC)  
**Website:** https://www.onlinequrancollege.com  
**Repository:** https://github.com/Ehtisham1239/app.com  
**Prepared:** 2 November 2026  
**Operating horizon:** 12 months; first 90 days are the controlled implementation window  
**Status:** Strategy approved for implementation; live performance baselines and analytics connections remain unverified.

## 1. Executive mandate

Build one integrated, evidence-led growth system for OQC. The objective is not traffic for its own sake. The objective is to attract the right parents, adult learners and families; help them make an informed decision; convert qualified enquiries into attended trials and paid enrolments; and retain learners through reliable academic delivery.

**North-star outcome:** qualified, attended trial bookings that progress to paid enrolments and remain active.

**Operating sequence:** Standardise → Verify → Publish → Measure → Improve.

No ranking, AI citation, lead volume, conversion rate or revenue outcome is guaranteed. Targets below are operating targets to establish and calibrate after a measured baseline, not public promises.

## 2. Strategic pillars

1. **Technical discoverability:** crawlability, indexation, canonical integrity, internal links, mobile experience, accessibility, performance and accurate structured data.
2. **Search intent coverage:** build useful course, audience, country and learning-question journeys; avoid near-duplicate city pages and keyword-only content.
3. **Answer-engine readiness:** publish concise, accurate answers supported by useful detail, clear authorship/review where appropriate, transparent sources and crawlable HTML.
4. **Brand trust:** one consistent online-only organisation identity, accurate contact details, authentic reviews, clear policies, transparent fees and verified claims.
5. **Conversion design:** make course fit, learner level, class format, teacher availability, time-zone coordination, trial terms and next steps easy to understand.
6. **Admissions operations:** respond consistently, qualify enquiries, track every stage, record lost reasons and follow up respectfully.
7. **Academic authority:** original learning guidance, teacher development evidence and scholar review for religious rulings or sensitive Islamic claims.
8. **Retention and referrals:** parent feedback, progress communication, attendance and service recovery should strengthen lifetime value and authentic advocacy.

## 4. Audience and intent architecture

### Parent / guardian
Questions: Is this suitable for my child? What happens in a lesson? How do I know the level? Can class times work with school and family routines? What does it cost? How is progress communicated?

### Adult learner
Questions: Can I start as a beginner? Can I improve recitation? Can I learn after work? Which course fits my current level?

### Hifz learner / family
Questions: What is the revision approach? How is consistency supported? How will progress and workload be discussed?

### Revert / new learner
Questions: Can I learn Arabic letters and basic recitation from the beginning? Can explanations be paced gently? Which language support is actually available?

Do not infer or publish demographic, language, gender or qualification claims unless the relevant service data is confirmed.

## 5. Search and content architecture

### Core topic clusters
- Online Quran classes: service overview, how online lessons work, parent decision guide.
- Noorani Qaida / beginner reading: readiness, lesson progression, common letter-recognition difficulties.
- Tajweed: foundations, makharij, common recitation errors, practice and revision.
- Hifz: memorisation planning, revision routines, family support and realistic expectations.
- Quran for children: age-appropriate learning routines, parent involvement, attention and lesson preparation.
- Quran for adults: beginner pathways, confidence, flexible study habits.
- Arabic / Islamic studies: publish only where the actual course, syllabus and teacher capability are confirmed.
- Choosing an online academy: questions to ask, fee transparency, teacher fit, trial evaluation and safeguarding expectations.

### Editorial rules
- Every page has one primary audience, one intent and one useful action.
- Start with a direct answer, then provide context, practical steps, examples and relevant next links.
- Add original OQC insight: sample lesson structure, learner progress method, parent checklist, practice worksheet or reviewed teaching explanation.
- Cite reliable primary sources for factual or religious claims. Have a qualified scholar review fatwa-level content; do not present the marketing team or AI as a Mufti.
- Use real author/reviewer identities and qualifications only after verification and permission.
- Disclose meaningful AI assistance where a reader would reasonably expect to know how content was produced.
- AI may research, outline, proofread and assist; it must not autonomously publish.
- Do not create thin pages to target every keyword, country, city or spelling variation.
- Existing editorial rule: keep blog posts concise (normally no more than 1,000 words unless the owner approves an exception); completeness and usefulness matter more than a target word count.
- Do not change publication dates unless a substantive update is made; keep an editorial change log.

### First 90-day content programme

2. Improve course pages with level, intended learner, lesson format, learning outcomes, prerequisites, sample progression, FAQs and trial CTA.
3. Publish a parent decision guide: what to check before choosing an online Quran academy.
4. Publish a beginner guide: how to start learning Quran reading as an adult or child.
5. Publish a practical Tajweed guide reviewed by a qualified reviewer.

7. Publish a parent lesson-preparation checklist and downloadable practice resource.
8. Refresh existing blogs only when the update adds accuracy, originality, usefulness or better internal navigation.

Each brief must include: audience, intent, primary query family, secondary questions, evidence sources, author, reviewer, unique OQC contribution, internal links, CTA, factual-claim checklist and publish/refresh decision.

## 6. Conversion and admissions system

### Website journey
Search / referral → relevant landing page → course or parent guide → free-trial information → enquiry form → confirmation → admissions contact → teacher/time availability confirmation → trial scheduled → trial attended → follow-up → paid enrolment → onboarding → retention.

### Conversion page requirements
- Explain exactly what the 3-day free trial means, eligibility, lesson frequency/duration and any conditions only after confirming operational policy.
- Explain what information is needed and why.
- Do not promise a response time unless Admissions has an approved service-level commitment and can measure it.
- Do not promise a teacher match or a specific time before availability is confirmed.
- Show a clear privacy notice and consent choices; collect only necessary data.
- Offer a useful confirmation state and explain the next step.
- Keep one primary CTA per page; secondary links should support, not compete with, the action.

### Admissions pipeline definitions
1. New enquiry
2. Contact attempted
3. Contacted
4. Qualified
5. Course / learner fit established
6. Teacher availability checked
7. Trial proposed
8. Trial booked
9. Trial attended
10. Follow-up due
11. Enrolled / paid
12. Not proceeding / nurture

Every non-conversion needs a reason code: no response, schedule mismatch, fee mismatch, course mismatch, teacher availability, timing, duplicate, not eligible, other. Do not mark an enquiry as a lead conversion merely because a form was submitted.

## 7. Measurement and KPI tree

### North-star
**Paid enrolments from qualified attended trials**, reported by country, course and acquisition source.

### Funnel measures
- Organic sessions and engaged landing-page visits
- Search impressions, clicks, CTR and average position by query/page/country
- AI / generative search visibility where Search Console reports it
- Trial form starts and completed valid submissions
- Qualified enquiry rate
- Contact rate and median time to first human response
- Trial booking rate and attendance rate
- Trial-to-paid conversion
- Paid enrolments, revenue, refunds and outstanding fees
- 30/60/90-day retention and learner churn
- Referral enquiries and referral-to-enrolment rate

### Commercial measures
- CPL = acquisition spend / valid enquiries
- CPA = acquisition spend / paid enrolments
- CAC = attributable acquisition cost / new paying learners
- LTV = contribution margin per learner over the observed lifetime
- LTV:CAC = learner lifetime value / acquisition cost
- Revenue per active learner
- Teacher capacity utilisation

Never report a metric without its definition, date range, source, denominator and owner. Segment paid and organic acquisition. Avoid vanity-only reporting.

### Measurement implementation order
1. Verify Google Search Console property ownership and sitemap submission.
2. Connect GA4 and GTM using least-privilege access and consent-aware configuration.
3. Define event taxonomy before tags are published.
4. Test events in preview/debug mode and compare with backend form records.
5. Establish country/course/source attribution and UTM naming standards.
6. Build a weekly funnel dashboard and monthly cohort review.
7. Add CRM/ERP stage reconciliation so website submissions are not mistaken for admissions or revenue.

**Suggested event taxonomy:** view_course, view_country_hub, trial_form_start, trial_form_submit, contact_form_submit, admissions_qualified, trial_booked, trial_attended, enrolment_paid. Events representing backend outcomes must be sent from the trusted application/CRM layer, not fabricated by browser clicks.

## 8. Technical SEO and answer-engine controls

- Maintain a single preferred HTTPS host and canonical URLs.
- Keep robots.txt and XML sitemap accurate; noindex private, duplicate, thin or utility pages as appropriate.
- Ensure every indexable page is internally linked and has a useful purpose.
- Use semantic HTML, descriptive title/description, one clear page H1, useful H2s and accessible link text.
- Use structured data only when it accurately reflects visible content. Keep Organization/EducationalOrganization details factual. Do not add a false LocalBusiness address, fake branch, fabricated review aggregate or unearned accreditation.
- FAQ content may help readers; do not promise FAQ rich results.
- Keep images responsive, compressed, correctly sized and provided with meaningful alt text where informative.
- Measure Core Web Vitals with field data where available; prioritise real-user problems over synthetic score chasing.
- Test mobile form completion, keyboard access, contrast, labels and error recovery.
- Maintain correct 404/410 responses and one-to-one redirects only for genuine moved equivalents.
- Make important page content available in crawlable HTML; avoid hiding essential content behind client-only interactions.
- PerplexityBot allowance is already present in robots configuration; recheck crawler guidance before changing bot rules.
- Do not add speculative AI schema, keyword stuffing, hidden prompt text, fake citations, or an llms.txt file as a claimed Google ranking requirement.

## 9. Brand trust, citations and reputation

- Use the exact legal/brand identity consistently.
- Online-only model must be explicit. Do not create Google Business Profiles or local branch listings that imply walk-in service.
- Keep official contact details consistent: info@onlinequrancollege.com and the verified published UK contact number. Do not invent a US phone number or local address.
- Build legitimate references through relevant education/community partnerships, expert contributions, interviews, useful resources and authentic editorial mentions.
- Citation tracker fields: publisher, country, URL, entity/name used, link status, date submitted, date verified, correction needed, owner and evidence.
- Do not bulk-submit to low-quality directories, buy links, exchange links at scale or use automated comment/forum links.
- Ask for honest feedback from real customers using a neutral request. Do not incentivise positive reviews, suppress negative reviews or fabricate AI-generated testimonials.
- Route negative feedback to a human owner; acknowledge, investigate and resolve. Never auto-post a response containing private learner information.

## 13. Governance and quality gates

Every public change must pass:
1. Factual accuracy and claim verification.
2. Islamic/academic review where required.
3. Brand and accessibility review.
4. SEO intent and internal-link review.
5. Privacy and data-minimisation review.
6. Mobile and form QA.
7. Production build and smoke tests.
8. Deployment confirmation and post-release check.

**Known / Assumed / Estimated / Unknown** must be used in reports. Unknown data must remain unknown until verified.

## 14. Operating dashboard: initial targets to calibrate

The first two weeks establish the baseline. After baseline, leadership sets targets based on real capacity and economics. Initial operational standards:
- 100% of public claims have an evidence owner.
- 100% of indexable priority pages have a defined audience, intent and conversion path.
- 100% of valid trial submissions are traceable to an Admissions record.
- 100% of paid enrolments have a source classification where attribution is available.
- 0 fabricated reviews, credentials, local offices or guaranteed outcomes.
- 0 mass-produced thin pages published without human review.
- 100% of production releases pass the defined quality gate.

These are governance standards, not ranking or revenue guarantees.

## 16. Definition of done

The operating system is considered implemented when:
- The website has verified technical foundations and clean indexable page architecture.
- Priority country and course pages offer distinct, useful, accurate information.
- Trial and enquiry journeys are clear, accessible and tested.
- Website events reconcile with real admissions and enrolment records.
- Admissions stages, follow-up owners and lost reasons are consistently recorded.
- Content has genuine human authorship/review and source evidence where required.
- Citations and reputation activity are authentic and tracked.
- Weekly and monthly reviews drive documented changes.
- Results are reported from evidence, not estimates presented as facts.

**Leadership rule:** optimise for qualified learners served well, not for publishing volume or ranking screenshots.
