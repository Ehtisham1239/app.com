Hifz is a long commitment, and families often ask when a child is ready to begin. There is no single age that suits every learner. This guide lists practical questions to discuss with a teacher before starting, so the decision is based on the learner’s reading, routine and support at home rather than on a course name.

Start with how the learner reads now

The Hifz course is described for learners who can already read the Quran. Before memorising, ask the teacher to listen to the learner read a short passage and explain what they notice. A learner who still hesitates over letters or joins may benefit from more time in Noorani Qaida or Quran Nazara first. Reading accurately before memorising makes later correction easier.

Questions to ask in the first conversation

  • What does the teacher need to hear before recommending Hifz?
  • How is new memorisation balanced with revision of earlier portions?
  • How long are lessons, and how much practice is expected between them?
  • How will progress and difficulties be shared with the family?
  • What happens if the learner needs to slow down for school exams or illness?

Write the answers down. They help you compare a realistic plan with your family’s week.

Look honestly at the weekly routine

Memorisation depends on regular revision as well as new lessons. Before starting, map a normal week: school or work, homework, travel and rest. Choose a short daily practice time that can be kept on busy days, not only on good ones. The guide to planning Hifz revision alongside school gives ideas for protecting that time.

Decide how a parent will support practice

For a child, a parent or guardian usually helps keep practice regular. Ask the teacher what support is useful at home: listening to a revision portion, keeping a simple record, or reminding the child of the agreed time. A parent does not need to be a teacher, but should know what the teacher has asked for that week.

Agree a review point, not a finishing date

Learners progress at different speeds. Instead of fixing a completion date, agree when the teacher and family will review how the routine is working — for example after the first few weeks. Use that conversation to adjust the amount of new memorisation, the revision plan or the timetable. This guide does not promise a pace, qualification or completion time.

When you are ready, request a trial conversation and mention that you are considering Hifz, the learner’s current reading level and the times that suit your family.
