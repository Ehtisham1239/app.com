Reading a translation is often the first step towards understanding the Quran in a language you know well. Tafseer — explanation of the meaning and context of verses by qualified scholars — goes further. This guide suggests how beginners can approach that study with a teacher, without relying on isolated phrases.

Know what a translation can and cannot do

Every translation is an interpretation of meaning, shaped by the translator’s choices. Different translations can word the same verse differently. Ask your teacher which translation the course uses and why, and treat it as a guide to the meaning rather than a replacement for the Arabic text.

Why explanation and context matter

Some verses are better understood with their context, the surrounding passage and explanation from established scholarship. A beginner reading alone may draw conclusions a qualified teacher would explain differently. The Quran Translation and Tafseer course gives you a teacher to ask when a passage is unclear.

A simple way to begin

  • Agree a short section to study with your teacher instead of starting at random.
  • Read the passage in Arabic, even slowly, and then the translation.
  • Note words or ideas you do not understand and bring them to the lesson.
  • Listen to the teacher’s explanation before drawing your own conclusions.
  • Review the same passage a few days later to see what you remember.

Keep a study notebook

A notebook helps you keep track of questions, new vocabulary and the teacher’s explanations. Over time it shows which themes and words recur. Keep your own reflections separate from what the teacher explained, so you can see which ideas came from qualified explanation.

Decide whether to add Arabic study

Many learners find that understanding meaning makes them want to recognise the Arabic words themselves. If so, discuss the Arabic Language course; the guide to translation and Arabic learning goals explains the difference.

Ask questions in the right place

Questions about religious rulings or practice should go to a suitably qualified scholar or teacher. Ask the college how such questions are handled in lessons. This guide is an introduction to how to study, not a source of religious rulings.

When you are ready, request a trial conversation and tell the coordinator which language you would like explanations in and what you have read so far.
