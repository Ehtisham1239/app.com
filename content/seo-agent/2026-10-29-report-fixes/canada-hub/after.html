Build a plan around your Canadian household

Start with the learner’s current skills and the part of the week that is realistically free. A young child who is still recognising Arabic letters needs a different conversation from a teenager fitting Hifz revision around school, homework and activities. Adult beginners can explain whether they are starting from the alphabet, returning after a long break or wanting help to read more accurately. You do not need to know the right level before asking.

Write down two or three possible lesson windows, allowing time to settle in after school or work. A quiet, repeatable slot is usually easier to keep through a busy term than one squeezed between other commitments. The coordinator discusses those preferences and confirms what is actually available; this page does not promise a teacher at every requested time.

Canada does not run on one clock

Families in Vancouver, Calgary, Toronto, Montreal, Halifax and St. John’s can be several hours apart, and Newfoundland’s local time differs from its neighbours by half an hour. Provinces also set their own seasonal clock rules, so a time difference that is correct this week may not be correct after a clock change. The planner on this page uses the selected city’s actual zone for the date you choose instead of applying one national rule.

If you are arranging lessons with a teacher or relative in another country, give the calendar date as well as the hour. Ask whether a recurring lesson will keep the same local time for the learner when clocks change, who confirms the updated arrangement and how you will be told. If your city is not listed, write its name on the enquiry rather than picking a nearby city and assuming the time is identical. The official sources linked further down this page explain the current provincial rules.

Choose the learning goal before the package

Explore Noorani Qaida for first letters and reading foundations, or Quran Nazara for guided reading of the Quran. The Tajweed guide explains how pronunciation feedback becomes part of regular practice. For memorisation, review the Hifz course and the guide to planning Hifz revision alongside school.

Learners who want to understand meaning can compare Quran Translation with the Arabic Language course; this comparison of the two goals can help you decide where to start. Ask the coordinator which course matches the learner’s age, reading level and aims.

CAD quotes are confirmed, not converted

Where the college has published an approved CAD price, the package section below shows it. If no CAD amount is shown, request a written quote before enrolling. We do not turn a USD price into a CAD fee with an assumed exchange rate. A quote should state the currency, billing period, lesson length and number of lessons.

Ask how payments, cancellations, rescheduling and any family offer apply to the package you are considering, and read the current terms and refund policy. The three-day free trial applies to eligible plans; the Weekend plan is excluded. No local price, discount or exception is created by this guide.

Prepare for the first conversation

For a child, a parent or guardian should complete the request. Tell the team what the learner already recognises or reads, which language helps them follow instructions — English, French, Urdu, Arabic or another language — and whether there are practical learning needs to discuss. Teaching-language availability is confirmed per teacher. Keep sensitive documents out of the general enquiry form.

Before the trial, check the device, sound and internet connection and choose a space where the learner can hear clearly. Afterwards, discuss the teacher’s recommendations and the real timetable before choosing a package.
